## Abstract

We introduce PineSORT, a novel Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) system for drone-based agricultural monitoring, speci  cally tracking pineapples for yield estimation. Our approach tackles key challenges such as repetitive patterns, similar object appearances, low frame rates, and drone motion effects. PineSORT enhances the tracking accuracy with motion direction cost, camera motion compensation, a three-stage association strategy, and overlap management. To handle large displacements, we propose an ORBbased camera compensation technique that signi  cantly improves the Association Accuracy (AssA). Evaluated via 5-fold cross-validation against BoTSORT and AgriSORT, PineSORT achieves statistically signi  cant gains in our Identity-Switch Penalized IDF1 (ISP-IDF1) metric, along with gains in IDF1 (Identity F1 Score), HOTA (Higher Order Tracking Accuracy) and AssA. These results con  rm its effectiveness in tracking low-FPS drone footage, making it a valuable tool for precision agriculture.

## 1. Introduction

Multiple Object Tracking (MOT) is a fundamental task in computer vision that involves detecting and tracking multiple objects across a sequence of video frames. The goal is to determine the object locations in each frame while maintaining their identities over time, even as they move, interact, or become occluded temporarily. MOT has broad applications in  elds such as action recognition, pose estimation \[ 28 \], surveillance \[ 2 \], autonomous driving \[ 40 \], sports analysis \[ 9 \], and human-computer interaction. However, their application in agricultural environments, particularly for crop monitoring using drones, presents unique challenges that require specialized solutions. Drones have become valuable tools in agriculture owing to their ability to capture high-resolution data over large areas. However, applying MOT in agricultural  elds in-

troduces several challenges related to the characteristics of both the environment and objects being tracked. For instance, pineapples, similar to many agricultural products, exhibit high visual similarity and are arranged in repetitive patterns, making it dif  cult to distinguish individual instances accurately. Additionally, occlusions caused by leaves or other vegetation can partially or completely obscure fruits. These factors increase the likelihood of identity switches in tracking algorithms and reduce reliability. Further complexity arises because of the motion of the drone. Even in cases where the drone moves in a single direction while the pineapples remain stationary, the objects in the image undergo continuous changes in position, scale, and perspective. Motion blur and changes in the frame rate can exacerbate tracking errors, particularly when the drone moves at high speeds, leading to inconsistent object associations. Accurate MOT in agriculture is crucial for applications such as yield estimation, harvesting automation, and crop monitoring. However, several challenges hinder realworld implementation, including occlusions, dense object arrangements, and redundant detections. The latter occurs when multiple bounding boxes are generated for a single object, leading to tracking inconsistencies. Moreover, the sparse distribution of pineapples in  elds, with large empty spaces between them, adds another layer of dif  culty. Traditional tracking approaches often struggle with scale variations, occlusions, and high visual similarity among objects. To tackle these challenges, it is crucial to develop advanced MOT methods that can effectively handle dynamic scenes caused by drone movement while maintaining realtime performance. These methods have to be robust against occlusions, redundant detections, and the repetitive patterns commonly found in pineapple  elds. Overcoming these limitations will enhance MOT systems, improving agricultural monitoring and automation. This, in turn, enables more ef  cient and precise crop management. ## 2. Related work 2.1. Multiple Object Tracking

The Simple Online and Realtime Tracking (SORT) \[ 6 \] family of algorithms has become a cornerstone in MOT, employing a tracking-by-detection paradigm that involves detection and association stages. In the detection stage, object detectors like YOLO \[ 29 \], Faster R-CNN \[ 31 \], or SSD \[ 22 \] localize objects in each frame using bounding boxes, while the association stage links detections across frames using the Hungarian algorithm \[ 20 \]. SORT variants have evolved to address various MOT challenges. DeepSORT \[ 36 \] enhances SORT by incorporating appearance information, while ByteTrack \[ 41 \] introduces a hierarchical data association strategy to leverage low-score detections. BoTSORT \[ 1 \] integrates motion and appearance cues with camera-motion compensation, and StrongSORT \[ 11 \] improves DeepSORT with tools like AFLink for track recovery and GSI for handling temporary object losses. OCSORT \[ 8 \] addresses Kalman  lter limitations by incorporating observation-centric corrections, and DeepOCSORT \[ 24 \] further integrates appearance cues. HybridSORT \[ 39 \] leverages both strong and weak cues for improved performance in occlusion and clustering scenarios. Finally, SFSORT \[ 26 \] introduces the Bounding Box Similarity Index, replacing the Kalman Filter. The SORT family is favored for its simplicity and adaptability in MOT tasks. 2.2. Multiple Object Tracking in Agriculture

There have been many recent advancement in precision agriculture \[ 12 - 14, 16, 38 \]. Regarding multiple object tracking, it has focused on addressing challenges speci  c to

dynamic  eld environments. NTrack \[ 27 \], a novel framework, utilizes dense optical  ow and particle  ltering to track cotton bolls, outperforming existing methods. Another approach employs fusion point cloud data and an improved Graph Neural Network (GNN) method for object tracking in farmlands, achieving high accuracy and precision \[ 17 \]. An EKF-based multi-object tracking framework has been developed for mobile robots in precision agriculture, speci  cally for tracking table-grape bunches in vineyards \[ 4 \]. AgriSORT, a simple online real-time trackingby-detection pipeline, focuses on ef  ciency and  exibility for agricultural applications, particularly in challenging environments like table grape vineyards \[ 34 \]. These methods enhance crop monitoring, yield prediction, and robotic harvesting but lack drone-based precision for yield estimation.

To address the impact of large displacements between consecutive frames caused by low frame rates (FPS), the system employs the ORB \[ 33 \] algorithm, with the implementation of OpenCV \[ 7 \]. This process consists of three main steps: keypoint detection, descriptor computation, and feature matching. In the  rst step, ORB utilizes the FAST algorithm for keypoint detection, which identi  es keypoints based on corner features within the image. For each detected keypoint, ORB computes a binary descriptor known as BRIEF, which is rotation-invariant and suitable for ef  cient matching. The  nal step involves matching features. The ORB descriptors can be matched using a brute-force approach. Let I t and I t  1 represent the current and previous frames, respectively. Feature matching is performed by calculating the Euclidean distance D ( p t,i, p t  1,j ) between descriptors d t and d t  1, thus identifying the set of matched keypoints M = \{ ( p t,i, p t  1,j ) \}. Using the matched keypoints M, the system computes the af  ne transformation matrix A, which maps the coordinates of keypoints from I t  1 to I t. The overlapping management mechanism computes the IoU between track bounding boxes B track and detection bounding boxes B det when creating a new track for detections not matched in the association steps. If the computed IoU exceeds a prede  ned threshold t, i.e., IoU ( B track, B det ) \> t, redundant detections are removed to prevent ID switches. This mechanism is particularly critical in sparse agricultural environments, where overlapping objects are uncommon but can still occur due to detection errors, occlusions, or perspective effects. By  ltering out redundant detections, the system enhances tracking accu-

racy and robustness, ensuring reliable performance in applications such as yield estimation and automated harvesting. The threshold t is empirically determined to optimize the trade-off between minimizing false positives and preserving valid detections, thereby maintaining the integrity of object trajectories.

## 4. Experiments

This section outlines the experimental setup, evaluation metrics, and benchmark results, comparing PineSORT to the baseline and analyzing its performance. 4.1. Dataset

The dataset includes 10 videos from a pineapple plantation from Upala Agricola \[ 3 \] (the world's largest exporter of fresh pineapple), recorded with a DJI Mavic 3 drone at 1920×1080 resolution. Videos capture crop rows from vertical (90°) and oblique (45°) angles, lasting 7-35 seconds (183-882 frames each). For MOT, we manually annotated the dataset using LabelStudio \[ 35 \]. The annotations were converted into the MOT format \[ 21 \], serving as the ground truth labels. The  nal annotations were exported at a frame rate of 25 Frame per second (FPS). 4.2. Evaluation Metrics

We evaluate tracking performance using TrackEval \[ 19 \], which provides comprehensive metrics. Overall performance is assessed with Multiple Object Tracking Accuracy (MOTA) \[ 5 \], considering false positives, missed detections, and identity mismatches, along with Identity F1 Score (IDF1) \[ 32 \] and Higher Order Tracking Accuracy (HOTA) \[ 23 \], the geometric mean of Detection Accuracy (DetA) and Association Accuracy (AssA). Given the importance of preserving object identities for yield estimation, we emphasize AssA, which measures identity consistency, and Identity Switches (IDSW) \[ 5 \], which counts identity switches. We also assess trajectorylevel metrics: Mostly tracked (MT) (trajectories tracked for  80% of their duration), Mostly lost (ML) (trajectories lost for \> 80% ), and Partially tracked (PT) (objects tracked for 20-80% of their lifespan). Additional measures include Identi  cation Precision (IDP) and Identi  cation Recall (IDR) for identity-based analysis. To account for identity preservation, we introduce the Identity-Switch Penalized IDF1 (ISP-IDF1) metric incorporating an exponential penalty based on IDSW. This normalization penalizes high ID switch counts while having a minimal effect when IDSW is low, for futher analysis in the evaluation dataset:

We use YOLOv11n \[ 18 \] as the detector for its speedaccuracy balance. The model was trained on the pineapple dataset using k-fold cross-validation, training on one video and evaluating on the rest. Training data included vertical, oblique, and combined camera orientations. 4.4. Trackers for comparison

We evaluate our approach against state-of-the-art (SOTA) SORT -based MOT methods, speci  cally SORT, ByteTrack, DeepOCSORT, StrongSORT, BoTSORT, and AgriSORT. In addition, to isolate the impact of motion-based tracking, we use BoTSORT without the Re-Identi  cation (ReID) module in our analysis. We also include HybridSORT and OCSORT, which improve robustness in occlusion and non-linear motion scenarios. Finally, we examine SFSORT, which replaces the KF with a custom cost function for better accuracy.

Additionally, AgriSORT replaces the Kalman  lter's transition matrix with an af  ne transformation between consecutive frames t  1 and t during the state prediction phase. The ef  cacy of this  ltering approach is highly contingent upon the congruence of the af  ne matrix with the underlying system dynamics. Discrepancies in the af  ne matrix can lead to signi  cant degradation of track predictions, particularly in low FPS scenarios, where increased matching errors and challenges in accurately capturing motion components-such as translation, rotation, and scaling-culminate in heightened estimation errors. Furthermore, the optical  ow technique operates under the assumption of small camera displacements, which limits its effectiveness in scenarios involving substantial motion. As observed in Table 2, SFSORT exhibits a considerable number of identity switches, resulting in a lower IDR compared to other trackers. This indicates its inadequacy in accurately maintaining object identities across frames in accordance with the ground truth. These performance metrics highlight the limitations of its cost index function, which relies solely on object bounding box characteristics and lacks integration of a Kalman  lter for motion prediction. The absence of a Kalman  lter compromises temporal consistency, leading to an overreliance on shape similarity, distance, and bounding box overlap. When an object is only partially visible upon entering or exiting the frame, its size may be underestimated, causing a decline in shape similarity scores as the object becomes fully visible in subsequent frames. Moreover, larger displacements between frames at FPS further increase the likelihood of identity switches, as they contribute to a decrease in bounding box overlap. BoTSORT demonstrates a lower ID switch rate compared to most other tracking methods, with the exception of PineSORT. However, it still experiences some ID switches due to its reliance on sparse optical  ow for feature track-

ing and RANSAC for af  ne matrix estimation. While this approach aims to accurately estimate background motion, the inherent assumptions of optical  ow may not adequately capture large displacements between frames. We anticipated that StrongSORT and DeepOCSORT, which depend on feature embeddings to enhance object association between tracks and predictions, would perform poorly since they were not trained on our dataset for feature embeddings comparison. StrongSORT, which relies heavily on appearance features, exhibits a high frequency of ID switches in our scenario, particularly when tracking homogeneous objects, leading to mismatches and inaccuracies, especially at low FPS with signi  cant object displacement. In contrast, DeepOCSORT demonstrates improved performance over StrongSORT, likely due to the introduction of Camera Motion Compensation using optical  ow and RANSAC, resulting in a substantial difference in the ISP-IDF1 metric. There is not a statistical difference between the trackers HybridSORT and OCSORT indicating similar performance for our benchmark in agricultural applications, as both tracking algorithms are predicated on the same SORT framework. Given the unidirectional trajectory of the drone, the implementation of camera compensation is critical for accurately capturing the dynamics of this movement. In instances where the object detector fails to identify a target in the subsequent frame, it may inadvertently assign new identi  ers to newly detected objects in the next frame, while neglecting to update the states of the previously tracked objects. The tracking algorithms that do not integrate this feature include SORT, HybriSORT, OC-SORT, ByteTrack, and StrongSORT. Conversely, AgriSORT, BoTSORT, and PineSORT uniquely incorporate camera compensation techniques to facilitate the updating and correction of object states based on camera movement. This observation suggests that recording agricultural  elds using a drone camera in a vertical orientation optimally facilitates the detection and tracking of objects, resulting in reduced variability and improved distinguishability of the target objects. ## 6. Conclusion

In this paper, we introduced PineSORT, a novel MOT tracker designed for drone videos in agricultural settings. The framework integrates key components such as a threestage association process, motion direction cost, camera compensation, and overlap management, signi  cantly improving tracking performance, especially in dynamic conditions with rapid object movement and low frame rates. We improved bounding box predictions and reduced identity switches in dense environments by integrating a KF with a constant-velocity model and expanding the state vector to include width and height. Tested on drone footage from a pinneaple plantation case, PineSORT leverages ORB for effective camera compensation in low FPS drone footage, outperforming traditional optical  ow method. PineSORT consistently outperformed baseline trackers, including BoTSORT, in terms of IDF1 and AssA scores, demonstrating its robustness in agricultural object tracking. Overall, PineSORT provides a reliable solution for agricultural tracking, laying the groundwork for future developments in yield estimation and automated harvesting, with a focus on scalability and improving performance in more challenging environments.
