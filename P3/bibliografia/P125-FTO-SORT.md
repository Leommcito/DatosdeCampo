# A B S T R A C T

As the importance of animal welfare and agricultural automation continues to grow, advancements in real-time multi-object tracking technology within pig farm environments have become essential. In commercial farms (unseen datasets) differing from the trained dataset, various factors, such as light flares, object overlap, and ambiguous boundaries between the foreground and background, pose challenges that reduce detection accuracy. Although methods using large models or ensemble detectors exist, they often suffer from slower execution speeds. While other studies focus on improving accuracy on seen datasets, this can result in lower accuracy when applied to different farm environments. To address this, our study introduces a new data augmentation method called FBDA (Foreground-Background Separation and Data Augmentation) using SAM and LaMa to enhance performance without affecting execution speed. We also implemented a new weighting technique in the existing loss function to handle overlaps or ambiguous boundaries, termed OFBL (Overlap and Foreground-Background Difference Loss), which improves detection performance while maintaining YOLO ' s speed. To further improve tracking performance, we introduced a new module, FTO (Farm Track-id Optimizer), into the BoT-SORT (Bag of Tricks for MOT Simple Online and Realtime Tracking) model, resulting in FTO-SORT (Farm Track-id Optimizer with SORT). Using our proposed method, we achieved significant improvements in tracking performance on unseen datasets, increasing IDF1 from 75.1% to 90.2% with YOLOv8 and from 68.7% to 86.7% with YOLOv11, representing gains of 15.1% and 18.0%, respectively. Additionally, by removing the Re-ID module from FTOSORT, the FPS on the TX2 board increased, achieving speeds that were approximately 10.3 times faster (from 0.6 to 6.2 FPS) and 11.1 times faster (from 0.6 to 6.7 FPS), significantly enhancing processing speed. We shared our tracking dataset at <https://github.com/YuSeungHyun97/fto-sort> for precision livestock farming research community. **1.** ** ** **Introduction**

- First, we propose a new data augmentation method called FBDA. Finally, some studies utilize the consistent number of pigs within a camera ' s view to improve tracking performance ( Yang et al., 2024b ). These studies contribute to animal welfare and agricultural automation by advancing pig tracking and detection technology, suggesting further potential for development in these fields. In summary, data augmentation techniques using SAM and LaMa are essential for maximizing model performance in complex environments like pig detection. By creating more refined and complex datasets, they surpass the limitations of simple augmentation methods, significantly boosting model performance in real-world applications. Complementary to data augmentation, loss function optimization further boosts detection robustness by addressing specific challenges such as overlapping objects or class imbalance. Previous studies propose various methods to optimize loss components related to object size, position, and classification. For instance, the SIoU (SCYLLA-Intersection over Union) ( Gevorgyan, 2022 ) loss function extends traditional IoU loss by incorporating four cost elements: angle, distance, shape, and IoU. Specifically, the angle cost enables more precise positional predictions by accounting for box inclination. Additionally, methods like Dynamic Multi-Scale Loss Optimization help models effectively detect objects of varying sizes ( Luo et al., 2023 ). This approach dynamically adjusts the loss based on object size and characteristics, assigning higher weights to small or challenging objects to maximize model performance. Such dynamic optimization techniques enhance model generalization across diverse environments ( Oksuz et al., 2020 ). Ultimately, improving loss functions plays a pivotal role in boosting the performance of object detection models. For example, ByteTrack ( Zhang et al., 2022 ) leverages a technique for adjusting detection confidence, allowing it to utilize even low-confidence detections, significantly enhancing its performance in multi-object tracking. By processing information from all detected objects, ByteTrack minimizes missed detections and achieves high accuracy. Re-ID modules are designed to re-identify objects when they temporarily disappear from view or become occluded. CLIP-ReID ( Li et al., 2023b ) addresses the object Re-ID problem through multi-modal learning of images and text. In this study, we propose a data augmentation method called FBDA (Foreground-Background Separation and Data Augmentation), which utilizes foundation models such as SAM (Segment Anything Model) ( Kirillov et al., 2023 ) and LaMa (Large Mask Inpainting) ( Suvorov et al., 2022 ) to enhance the detection performance of the YOLO ( Ultralytics, 2020, 2024; Wang et al., 2024a; Wang et al., 2023; Wang et al., 2024b ) model. Additionally, we introduce OFBL (Overlap and ForegroundBackground Difference Loss), which is designed to account for object overlap and differences between foreground and background. To improve tracking performance, we combine the BoT-SORT (Byte-Object Tracking with SORT) model ( Wojke et al., 2017 ) with the FTO (Farm Track-id Optimizer) module, creating a new tracking system named FTO-SORT.

Fig. 3 provides a visual illustration of the overall flow of the proposed methodology, with the YOLO detector model trained on the German pig dataset ( Riekert et al., 2020 ). To enhance multi-object tracking accuracy during training, we apply the new data augmentation technique FBDA and employ OFBL as the loss function. In this process, objects classified as false negatives are separated into foreground and background and added to the dataset. Simultaneously, data augmented through the FBDA technique is used to improve detection performance. Finally, by removing the Re-ID module and incorporating the FTO module proposed in this study, we enhance the generalization performance of object tracking, thereby improving the overall accuracy of the tracking system.

*3.2.* * * *Overlap* * * *and* * * *foreground-background* * * *difference* * * *loss* * * *(OFBL)*

The YOLO model traditionally uses a loss function composed of Bounding Box Loss, Segmentation Loss, Classification Loss, and Objectness Loss to predict an object ' s location, boundary, class, and presence.

Loss YOLO = α ⋅ Loss BOX + β⋅ Loss SEG + γ⋅ Loss CLS + δ⋅ Loss DFL

limitations in accurately detecting objects with overlapping or complex boundaries, such as pigs. The default parameter values are set as α = 7.5, β = 7.5, γ = 1.5, and * δ* = 0.5, and these values remain fixed throughout the process.

Loss OFBD = Loss YOLO + λ⋅ Loss O + λ⋅ Loss FB

To address this issue, this paper proposes assigning higher weights to difficult-to-detect pigs (e.g., those with overlapping sizes or colors like the background) to emphasize them more in the learning process. The proposed method increases the weights for objects with high overlap and complex boundaries during training, enabling the generation of more accurate masks. Here, Loss O represents the loss related to object overlap, and Loss FB denotes the loss associated with pixel differences between the foreground and background. By setting λ = α + β + γ + δ, higher weights are applied to challenging objects, enhancing detection accuracy. Loss O increases the loss weight in proportion to the number of overlapping objects, enhancing the detection of overlapping objects. In Fig. 5, as the number of overlapping pigs increases, the weight visually shifts towards green, indicating an increase, while it shifts towards blue as the weight decreases. The weights range from 0 to a maximum of 2.5. Loss FB is based on pixel differences between the foreground and background, assigning a higher loss weight to objects with pixel values similar to the background, thereby improving detection performance. Fig. 6 visualizes the segmentation annotations, with the foreground

highlighted in pink and the background in green using bounding box annotations. To account for image downscaling by up to 32 pixels during training with the YOLO network, the background area is expanded by 32 pixels around the actual bounding box. In this setup, the loss weight assigned to objects with foreground-background similarity can reach up to 2. Ultimately, the proposed Loss OFBD enhances the accuracy of objects that are challenging to detect due to high overlap, contributing to improved TP, FP, and FN metrics, which are crucial for tracking applications.

*3.3.* * * *BoT-SORT* * * *and* * * *FTO* * * *unification*

This study uses the BoT-SORT model to track detected pigs. BoTSORT combines BoT (Bag of Tricks for MOT) with SORT (Simple Online and Realtime Tracking) to enhance track management and object Re-ID capabilities, significantly improving multi-object tracking performance. This integration enables continuous tracking of the positions and movements of pigs detected by the YOLO model. However, in complex scenes where multiple pigs overlap or exhibit irregular movements, the Re-ID module sometimes causes track confusion. The Re-ID module requires classification training, and its performance degrades on unseen datasets. Distinguishing individual pigs proves challenging, as, unlike humans, pigs lack distinct visual markers, such as clothing, making differentiation in farm environments more difficult. Unlike conventional methods that rely on Re-ID modules, FTO leverages the relatively stable number of pigs within each frame and adjusts track management accordingly. As illustrated in Fig. 7, objects with a confidence score of 0.25 or higher are counted in each frame, and the current pig count is compared with the average pig count from previous frames. Based on this comparison, tracks are modified or added dynamically. Blue blocks represent default tracks generated by the BoT-SORT model, while red and purple blocks denote newly created or modified tracks introduced by FTO. In addition to count-based adjustment, FTO incorporates a motionaware strategy during the initial 30 frames. It calculates the average movement of active tracks by measuring the normalized Euclidean distance between object positions, using the image diagonal as a scaling factor. If the average movement exceeds a predefined threshold (e.g., 0.001), FTO dynamically tunes several tracking parameters to improve robustness under high-motion conditions: the association threshold is increased to 0.9, and the threshold for new track initialization is raised

to 0.7. New tracks are only created when the detection confidence is sufficiently high, considering the overall motion level in the scene. This integrated mechanism suppresses unnecessary track creation and improves tracking stability in environments where object displacement is significant. Additionally, when the IoU between detected objects and existing tracks in the current frame falls below a predefined threshold, FTO attempts to reassign missed " Lost Tracks " using active tracks from the previous frame, enabling re-tracking of temporarily lost objects. Tracks that show no movement across consecutive frames - based on the 2norm criterion - are classified as " Unmove Tracks " and preserved to prevent premature termination. These combined strategies allow FTO to operate reliably even in dynamic, overlapping conditions without relying on Re-ID modules. **4.** ** ** **Experiments**

Automated monitoring of health and welfare for individual pig management has garnered growing attention in recent years. In this paper, we proposed the FBDA (Foreground-Background Separation and Data Augmentation) and OFBL (Overlap and Foreground-Background Difference Loss) techniques to improve detection accuracy on unseen datasets in the YOLO model. These techniques contributed to a notable enhancement in detection performance, achieving an increase of up to 6.4 % in AP, while maintaining the model ' s processing speed. The FBDA technique was particularly effective in separating the foreground and background, allowing the model to better generalize to complex and cluttered environments. Moreover, data augmentation through the OFBL technique improved the model ' s ability to handle overlapping objects by carefully considering the difference between the foreground and background, leading to more accurate detection of foreground objects. Additionally, this study introduced the FTO (Farm Track-id Optimizer) module, which significantly enhanced tracking performance by integrating it with the BoT-SORT algorithm, resulting in the FTO-SORT system. FTO-SORT demonstrated robust generalization capabilities on unseen datasets, achieving a remarkable 18.0 % improvement in IDF1. This improvement was particularly noticeable in complex farm environments where object overlap and motion variation are common. The combination of the FTO module and BoT-SORT strengthened the stability of the tracking system, enabling consistent tracking of pigs ' continuous movements despite environmental challenges. A key advantage of this approach was the elimination of the need for a Re-ID module, which substantially reduced computational overhead and improved processing efficiency. As a result, execution speed improved more than elevenfold, from 0.6 FPS to 6.7 FPS on the TX2 board, demonstrating the system ' s potential for real-time applications with more powerful embedded boards. The performance improvements obtained by the proposed method are expected to significantly impact large-scale smart farms by enabling more efficient and accurate monitoring and management of pigs in realtime. However, the proposed method does have certain limitations. First, despite the overall performance improvements achieved with the FTO module, the system ' s tracking accuracy decreased below 91 % when pigs were densely packed, revealing performance degradation in environments prone to overlap and confusion due to reduced interobject distances. Second, while the processing speed on the TX2 board improved to handle up to 6 frames per second, it still falls short of the real-time standard of 30 FPS. These limitations highlight the need for further optimization to enhance both processing speed and detection accuracy. Third, although the proposed method achieved substantial tracking improvements on the Jochiwon dataset with 1,260 annotated images, such improvements were not as pronounced on the Edinburgh University dataset. This suggests that the generalization performance may vary depending on dataset characteristics. Additionally, when combined with specific detectors, the proposed method exhibited lower MOTA scores compared to other trackers in scenarios involving large object displacements. While the proposed method consistently achieved the highest accuracy across all metrics when using the latest YOLOv11

detector, its MOTA score decreased when applied with YOLOv8. These findings indicate that further enhancements are necessary to improve robustness under highly dynamic tracking conditions and across varying detection backbones. To address these challenges, future research will focus on achieving higher real-time processing capabilities and improving detection accuracy by incorporating more efficient network architectures and advanced data augmentation techniques. Innovations in network design and training methodologies, along with improved hardware accelerators, may also contribute to meeting the real-time standard. By optimizing these aspects, the system can further support the automation of pig monitoring and management in large-scale smart farms. This research is expected to provide significant advancements in the adoption of AI-based systems in the livestock industry, paving the way for more scalable and sustainable smart farming practices. In addition, we plan to release both the source code and labeled dataset upon publication.
